# Morning perioperative report-out template

Configurable worked example adapted from the supplied morning meeting structure. Ask only the questions relevant to the organization. Capture reported facts, current barriers, mitigation, owner and next checkpoint. Do not infer clinical clearance, invent add-ons or make clinical decisions. Keep personal coaching matters out of a broadly shared operations workbook.

1. **On-time starts and rounding:** Were starts delayed? What did the reporting team identify as the cause, what mitigation was reported, and what follow-up could prevent recurrence? What rounding identified a near miss or useful standard-work improvement?
2. **Cardiac services:** Report call-outs, start delays, corrective follow-up, explicitly known pending cases and other documented concerns.
3. **Preoperative:** Report call-outs, remaining case flow, receiving/ICU coordination needs, handoff documentation, transport barriers, reported H&P/order/laterality concerns and mitigation. Include only known add-ons and documented late-room coverage needs.
4. **PACU:** Report call-outs, case-flow constraints, occupancy and receiving-bed information, transport needs, planned admissions and documented receiving arrangements. Separate available beds from anticipated discharges and future demand.
5. **OR charge:** Report breaks/lunch coverage, reported compliance/environment checks and defects, work orders or assigned corrections, rounding observations, rooms expected to continue at 3 PM/5 PM and coverage beyond call teams. Carry early-start notifications to the afternoon follow-up.
6. **SPD:** Report call-outs, turnover tray needs, vendor/representative tray status, delivery uncertainty and afternoon tray plans. A tray is not ready solely because a delivery was expected.
7. **Specialty coordinators and infection prevention:** Report special-case flow constraints, awareness items and outstanding follow-up within approved scope.
8. **Biomed:** Report broken beds, ESUs or specialty equipment; available backup if confirmed; service/work-order reference; ordered/received items; and unresolved or mitigated concerns.
9. **Throughput/support lead:** Report staffing gaps, bed/stretcher availability, team-huddle concerns, documented environmental/equipment defects and assigned ownership. Do not convert a missing check into a completed check.
10. **Patient-experience/documentation role:** Report operational chart-readiness concerns already communicated to the responsible team, planned rounding/discharge flow, reported mitigation and remaining follow-up. Use aggregated operational details where possible.
11. **Supply chain:** Report new backorders, confirmed implant availability, low-supply concerns, request responses and outstanding items.
12. **Assistant managers/service leaders:** Report preference-card/laterality concerns, case sequencing dependencies, equipment/supply needs for tomorrow, correct specialty room assignments, coordinator concerns and coverage over the next few days. Include documented extra-room utilization. Separate follow-up actions from unsupported recommendations.

For every extracted numeric metric, use the configured key, units, observed time and applicable operating date. For issues, use stable issue ID, original event date, validated severity or UNKNOWN, current state, evidence cue, and stated owner/action/due. Missing numeric observations are omitted; missing sources are logged explicitly. Report AM facts separately from later revisions. Use the common extraction prompt and version-2 record contract.
